# Unit performance ladder

The protocol and pass rules are in [ADR 0004](../../adr/0004-unit-simulation-architecture.md#performance-ladder), with the lane layout and movement cost in [ADR 0017](../../adr/0017-ladder-offset-lanes-and-movement-cost.md) and report-only crossing figures in [ADR 0018](../../adr/0018-crossing-figures-report-only.md). Scenario version 3 uses the native-array simulation and the instanced unit mesh the game currently draws (ADR 0016). The two armies spawn wholly on opposite sides of the centre seam, with their facing rows on their own goal lines. Seeded move orders alternate far ends every `OrderCycleSeconds` (20 simulation seconds), beginning on tick 1. The spread changes x only; each side keeps its goal-line z. The fixed layout has a 1.2-cell lane gap, spawn centres at x = −40 and +25, far ends at −120 and +120, and spread 7. Every parameter lives in `PerformanceLadder.asset`; no per-rung values or trial overrides exist.

Ended orders are renewed to the current cycle destination before the next movement step; live orders are never renewed early. The group-stall rule and all other movement rules remain enabled. Rule 2 (contact with an arrived member) cannot fire here: renewal makes idle members move before its next check. Its neighbour search still runs. Commands, spatial grid, flow fields and movement run at 30 Hz; matrices and instanced drawing update every frame. The active-order check includes batched ticks and rejects a gap longer than one tick. Targeting, combat and commander AI are not implemented. The first faction uses small units, the second large. Version 1 held position; its entries remain, and versions 1 and 3 are not a like-for-like series. Version 2 identifies the discarded head-on layout in the Issue #232 branch history.

Every Player reports both complete judged cycles in the window (ticks 601–1200 and 1201–1800). Pass share counts units changing sides of the other army's mean x. Meet share counts units coming within 2 cells of an opponent, using exact centre distances on every fifteenth tick and the existing spatial grid. Entries show each side's worst judged-cycle pass, meet and mean travel shares across valid attempts, plus cycle 0's travel share in its own column. Travel share is mean endpoint displacement in any direction divided by free travel: it misses motion that returns to its starting point and cannot distinguish sideways shoving from progress along the lane. Low shares are valid; missing or malformed counters are invalid. The window-close tick is saved before the drain. If it is below 1800, a fast release run is invalid; a slow run stays valid but unchecked unless its rung's medians pass, in which case it is replaced within the same six-attempt limit. Incomplete development cost runs are labelled unchecked.

Every entry and PR quoting its height or travel figures says **armies mostly stuck, movement unsolved** beside them. The label stays until a full ladder entry shows, at every rung and side in all three valid runs, travel share of at least 0.60 in cycles 0, 1 and 2, at least 90% passed and `ceil(sqrt(N/2))` met in cycles 1 and 2, and a later accepted ADR names that evidence and removes the words. That qualifying entry keeps its label. Frame times do not remove it. The current height describes active units mostly jammed on themselves; it does not establish smooth movement, the cost of free movement or mixing armies, or the vision target. A later entry that lowers a share quotes both entries' figures in its PR; the frame-time pass rule and regression checks stay unchanged.

Each attempt records `renewedOrdersByArrivalRule` for its measurement window, in rule order: 1 = exact goal, 2 = contact with an arrived member, 3 = goal-disc congestion, 4 = six-second group stall. Raw Player metadata also records warm-up renewals separately as `warmupRenewedOrdersByArrivalRule`. Drain-time renewals are excluded. Report these counts at the 2,000-unit rung alongside its timings.

Under [ADR 0009](../../adr/0009-slow-frame-regression-cutoff.md), a step is `regressed` when its average frame time median is worse than the comparison by more than both 10% and 0.5 ms, or its current 1% low median is at least 3.0 ms (including exactly 3.0 ms) and exceeds both of those thresholds. The average check is unchanged; the 1% low is still recorded below the cut-off.

Before measuring, read [Issue #97's speed-test finding](../issue-97-speed-test-finding.md), commit the measured sources, close the Editor, set Windows power mode to best performance and leave this PC idle. The Producer reserves the PC: no other Unity run may overlap a ladder. The Player requires a real 1920×1080 borderless desktop with focus. A sandbox without these cannot produce valid measurements; an offscreen render request is not a substitute.

From the repository root, build both release and development Windows x64 Mono Players. Studio agents use the launcher's `unity_batch` for every Editor run; the direct PowerShell form below is for the Producer. The pipe waits for the GUI executable to finish. The builder generates only its owned scene/scenario and leaves Editor Build Settings unchanged.

```powershell
& 'C:\Program Files\Unity\Hub\Editor\6000.3.23f1\Editor\Unity.exe' -batchmode -nographics -projectPath "$PWD\Game" -logFile "$PWD\Game\Logs\ladder-build.log" -executeMethod Northwatch.EditorTools.PerformanceLadderBuilder.BuildWindows -ladder-commit FULL_40_HEX_COMMIT_SHA -quit | Out-Null
```

Replace `FULL_40_HEX_COMMIT_SHA` with the Producer's full measured commit SHA. Check the build log before running. Changes to the scenario or harness start a new comparison series. A unit mesh change stays in the series (ADR 0016).

The runner refuses uncommitted sources, a build for another SHA, changed source hashes, or changes to sources/hardware during measurement. It runs every step in order, requesting three valid fresh Players within six attempts per step. Each Player warms up for 10 seconds, records 60 seconds and drains delayed timings for two seconds. The preallocated buffer holds one million frames; overflow invalidates the run. Callback timestamps use native Windows QueryPerformanceCounter, sharing the FrameTimingManager clock origin and frequency. Native frame-start timestamps match disjoint intervals between consecutive script callbacks; rendered frames are counted independently. Overlapping timing polls are deduplicated by timestamp; two distinct samples matching one frame invalidate it. Metadata records matched and unmatched sample counts; unmatched samples invalidate the run. Frames after the closed window are ignored during the drain, while delayed window samples still match. Missing samples remain missing after the drain. Setup failures log their reason and exit with code 1; named exceptions in Player logs also invalidate a run.

Raw CSV, metadata, Player logs and development captures stay under ignored `Game/Logs/`. The Player counts physical cores with Windows `GetLogicalProcessorInformationEx`; Unity supplies the CPU model, logical thread count, rendered GPU and memory sizes. The runner reads only GPU description/driver version and Windows product/version/build values from fixed registry locations, which work inside the sandbox without CIM access. It matches the driver to the rendered GPU and rejects missing or ambiguous information. Hardware is checked across attempts and registry values before and after the ladder. No device name or identifier is read. Public entries contain an allow-listed summary with all attempt results, three-run spread and optional timing missing counts. Comparison entries come from the local `main` ref's addition history; update that ref before launching the worktree. A failed step still records its curve; invalid runs, regressions and previously passing steps that fail give the command a nonzero exit code. Every entry is append-only and names the full measured commit; the Producer can commit the entry alone afterward.

Before the release ladder, run both development cost modes at 2,000 units, with no other Unity run active:

```powershell
& .\Game\Builds\PerformanceLadderDevelopment\PerformanceLadder.exe -ladder-units 2000 -ladder-profile -ladder-output "$PWD\Game\Logs\unit-ladder\profile" -logFile "$PWD\Game\Logs\unit-ladder\profile.log" | Out-Null
& .\Game\Builds\PerformanceLadderDevelopment\PerformanceLadder.exe -ladder-units 2000 -ladder-wait -ladder-output "$PWD\Game\Logs\unit-ladder\wait" -logFile "$PWD\Game\Logs\unit-ladder\wait.log" | Out-Null
```

Development captures run at **25 fps** and record the full 60-second window by default. Release measurements remain uncapped (`targetFrameRate=-1`) with the Profiler off. For a short diagnostic, add `-ladder-profile-seconds 10` (accepted range: 10–60 seconds, development captures only). This shortens binary recording; the Player still completes its usual warm-up, 60-second window and drain. Validate a capture change on a short capture before analyzing a full one. No argument-file fallback is used.

After both Players exit, run the Unity batch command with `-executeMethod Northwatch.EditorTools.LadderProfileAnalysis.Analyze` and a separate log. Analysis defaults to `Game/Logs/unit-ladder/profile.raw` and writes `profile.raw-summary.txt` and `profile.raw-summary.txt.json`. For another capture, pass `-ladder-capture-path Logs/unit-ladder/NAME.raw`; the normalized path must stay inside this project's `Logs/`. Analysis also reads the capture Player's sibling metadata to bind the cost to its build commit.

The worker analysis sums every retained frame and thread for the tick chain's named job markers, counting ticks from `BuildSpatialGridJob`. It reports mean ms/tick, nearest-rank 99th percentile over one-tick frames, per-job means, tick count, capture bytes and peak analysis memory. Per-frame selection, drawing and crossing jobs are listed separately and excluded from that total. The uncapped wait mode writes preallocated samples from `Northwatch.Simulation.Wait`, summed per frame, only for frames that completed a tick (ADR 0012 set C). The runner reports their median, 99th percentile, maximum and count. The capped capture's wait is not used. These are separate single development runs: on a non-reference PC they show trends only, not reference-PC headroom or future ORCA cost. They never change the release pass rule or regression check.

Then run the release ladder, giving it both cost outputs:

```powershell
node tools/unit-ladder.js "re-run: Issue 232 offset lanes" no Game/Logs/unit-ladder/profile.raw-summary.txt.json Game/Logs/unit-ladder/wait
```

Replace the context with `baseline`, `"PR #123"` or `"re-run: reason"`. The second argument is `yes` only for the ADR's RTX 3050 6 GB and mid-range six-core CPU; otherwise `no`. The runner refuses missing or stale cost data. If the full worker capture exceeds the unchanged 192 MiB limit, repeat with `-ladder-profile-seconds 40`, then 20, and report the duration. A short 10-second capture remains available for diagnostics, but cannot supply a ladder entry's worker cost.

Every reader scans all retained frames except the first and last, without striding or early success. Analysis rejects fewer than 200 samples or any managed allocation beneath `Northwatch.Simulation` or `Northwatch.Drawing`. Previously, the uncapped ladder file exceeded Unity's 2,000-frame retention limit: its 1,998 drawing samples covered only about 1.4 seconds at 1,400 fps. At 25 fps, the full window fits: #230 recorded 1,499 drawing samples and 2,998 simulation samples across 60 seconds. Each frame spans at least one 30 Hz tick, exercising the scheduling path; zero-tick frames take a subset of that path. The allocation checks therefore retain their coverage of the simulation/drawing paths and cover more elapsed time. Tests also verify an allocation present in just one interior frame. As before, this is evidence for the recorded workload, not a guarantee against allocations that depend on unrecorded frame counts.

Ladder, movement and sandbox readers refuse raw files larger than **192 MiB (201,326,592 bytes)** before loading. The previous 128 MiB guard rejected existing uncapped sandbox captures; #230 measured those unchanged files at 153,697,015–171,889,880 bytes, with the three-capture analysis peaking at 4,048,093,184 committed bytes. Sandbox capture duration, frame rate and validation workload stay unchanged. No frames are skipped to satisfy the byte guard. Oversized input fails without an allocation result; use a shorter ladder diagnostic or a supported movement capture rate, and investigate oversized sandbox input without changing its timing check.

Each capture gets a sibling **`CAPTURE.raw-memory.txt`** containing `stage`, `capture_bytes`, `peak_working_set_bytes` and `peak_committed_bytes`, read inside Unity with Windows process counters. Successful analysis requires both peaks below **16,000,000,000 bytes (16 GB)** and fails if counters are unavailable or zero. `before_load` only reports context; the budget is enforced after analysis. These are process-lifetime high-water marks, so later captures in a multi-capture job include earlier work. The budget check happens after loading and cannot interrupt native expansion; the byte guard is the protection before loading. Failed analysis releases frames and attempts an `analysis_incomplete` memory record without replacing the original error.

Keep the raw captures and memory files locally for QA. Development captures are not ladder measurements.
